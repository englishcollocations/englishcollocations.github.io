Lesson 35 – Movement & Speed

English has a number of words that mean "fast" – but each one has different collocations.

Use <strong>fast</strong> to describe a vehicle, person, or action: a <strong>fast car</strong>, a <strong>fast train</strong>, a <strong>fast runner</strong>, <strong>work fast</strong>, <strong>drive fast</strong>, <strong>learn fast</strong>

Use <strong>quick</strong> to describe an event or action: a <strong>quick lunch</strong>, a <strong>quick glance</strong>, a <strong>quick shower</strong>, a <strong>quick decision</strong>

Use <strong>rapid</strong> to describe a change: a <strong>rapid increase/decline</strong>, a <strong>rapid change</strong>, <strong>rapid growth</strong>, <strong>rapid progress</strong>

The word <strong>speedy</strong> is often used with recovery and resolution: a <strong>speedy recovery</strong> from surgery, a <strong>speedy resolution</strong> to the problem.

The word <strong>swift</strong> is often used with action, response, and reaction: The <strong>swift action</strong> of the police enabled the criminal to be caught immediately; we received a <strong>swift response</strong> to our request for information.

The word <strong>prompt</strong> means "fast and punctual" – it is often used with information and payment or customer service. For example, you can give a <strong>prompt reply</strong> to an e-mail, and receive <strong>prompt delivery</strong> of a product you ordered.

The word <strong>hasty</strong> means "fast and in a rush" – He made a <strong>hasty exit</strong> from the party when he saw his ex-girlfriend walk in. This word often has connotation of doing something without sufficient consideration. You might come to a <strong>hasty conclusion</strong> before considering all the evidence, and make a <strong>hasty decision</strong> that you'll regret later.

When you're driving, you need to pay attention to the <strong>speed limit</strong> – the maximum velocity permitted. If you're driving at <strong>breakneck speed</strong> (extremely fast), then you might need to <strong>slow down</strong>. If you get caught in a <strong>speed trap</strong> (where the police measure the velocity of passing cars), you might have to pay a <strong>speeding ticket</strong> (penalty for driving too fast).

Of course, you might need to <strong>put on a burst of speed</strong> (sudden increase in speed) to pass a car that's moving at a <strong>snail's pace</strong> (very slowly). If major construction is being done on the road, then traffic might <strong>slow to a crawl</strong> (slow down to a very slow speed) and eventually <strong>come to a standstill</strong> (stop completely). To talk about changes in speed, we can say something is <strong>gaining speed</strong> or <strong>speeding up</strong> (to describe moving faster) or <strong>losing speed</strong> or <strong>slowing down</strong> (to describe moving slower).

Finally, to talk about eating or walking in a slow and relaxed way, you can use the word "<strong>leisurely</strong>" – After a <strong>leisurely breakfast</strong>, we took a <strong>leisurely stroll</strong> through the park.
