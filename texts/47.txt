Lesson 47 – Praising & Criticizing

When I told my family I wanted to be a professional musician, I faced a <strong>barrage of criticism</strong> (intense attack of negative comments) from my parents, who strongly disapproved of the idea. I'd had a feeling that they would only <strong>give their blessing</strong> (give their approval/support) to a more "traditional" career track.

I <strong>countered their criticism</strong> (argued against their criticism) by pointing out that I'd rather follow my passion and enjoy my job than get rich working a job I hated. Although they gave their <strong>grudging approval</strong> (angry and reluctant approval) to my enrollment in music school, I could tell by my father's <strong>frown of disapproval</strong> (disapproving facial expression) that he wished I'd study law or medicine instead.

The only person who encouraged me was my aunt, who <strong>thinks the world of me</strong> (has great respect and admiration for me) and has always offered <strong>enthusiastic support</strong> for my hopes and dreams. She <strong>heartily approved</strong> (completely and sincerely approved) of my plans and dismissed my parents' criticisms as ridiculous.

My parents finally came around when I gave my first concert in Carnegie Hall, to a <strong>standing ovation</strong> (when the audience stands up and claps for an extended period of time). The critics had given my show <strong>rave reviews</strong> (extremely positive evaluations), describing it as an <strong>outstanding performance</strong> (an excellent, superior performance).

After seeing me on stage, my parents <strong>congratulated me warmly</strong> (congratulated me with love) and apologized for the <strong>scathing criticisms</strong> (very strong and hurtful criticisms) they had made in the past. Later, I heard them <strong>speaking highly of</strong> (saying good things about) my music to some of their friends. I had finally <strong>won their approval</strong>.
