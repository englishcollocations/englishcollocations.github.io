Lesson 44 – Deciding & Choosing

How do you <strong>make a decision</strong>? Do you make lists of <strong>pros and cons</strong> (advantages and disadvantages) and carefully <strong>consider all the options</strong>… or do you <strong>trust your intuition</strong> (listen to your deep, instinctive feelings) and then <strong>make a choice</strong> based on your feelings?

If it's an especially <strong>tough decision</strong>, you might want to <strong>ask for advice</strong> from a good friend. After discussing the issue <strong>in-depth</strong> (talking about it in a lot of detail) and listening to your friend's suggestions, you can choose to either <strong>take their advice</strong> (implement their advice) or <strong>ignore their advice</strong>.

When you make a decision very quickly, we call it a <strong>snap decision</strong>. That's somewhat similar to a <strong>spur-of-the-moment decision</strong> - a decision made spontaneously, without very much thinking or planning. If you do this, then you might need to <strong>defend your decision</strong> against people who believe it was an <strong>arbitrary decision</strong> (one determined by chance or impulse, not by logic or principle).

Some people remain <strong>undecided</strong> because they're afraid to make a <strong>costly mistake</strong> (a mistake with serious negative consequences). In this case, <strong>weighing the possible outcomes</strong> (analyzing the possible future results) can be helpful. Other people are always questioning themselves and having <strong>second thoughts</strong> (reconsidering, doubting your previous decision) about the choices they've already made.

Although it might seem attractive to <strong>keep your options open</strong> as long as possible, you run the risk of waiting so long that you end up <strong>having no choice</strong> (being limited to only one course of action). It's far better to develop <strong>sound judgment</strong> (the intelligent ability to decide things) so that you can <strong>make wise decisions</strong> (good and smart decisions) and be confident about them.
