Lesson 43 – Beliefs & Doubts

When you <strong>strongly/firmly believe</strong> something, we can call it a <strong>deeply-held belief</strong> or an <strong>unshakable belief</strong> – the latter phrase implies that it would be difficult to convince you to change your mind. It's possible to have a <strong>subconscious belief</strong> – a belief that you are not actively aware of. Your emotions can also <strong>color your judgment</strong> (influence your opinions/decisions).

A lot of people <strong>believe in superstitions</strong> – irrational beliefs that have no basis in fact, like the number 13 being unlucky. Others have a <strong>blind faith</strong> (believe in something without thinking) in fate or destiny. When new evidence appears, it can either <strong>strengthen/reinforce your belief</strong> (make it stronger) or <strong>undermine/weaken your belief</strong> (make it weaker).

When forming your beliefs, be careful about <strong>making assumptions</strong>, which can lead to <strong>erroneous conclusions</strong>. False beliefs held by many people are called <strong>common misconceptions</strong>. Scientists and other researchers often publish material to help <strong>dispel misconceptions</strong> (eliminate them) – for example, "Contrary to popular belief, it's not harmful to go swimming immediately after eating."

If you are completely sure about something, then you can describe it as "<strong>beyond/without a shadow of a doubt</strong>." But sometimes <strong>doubts arise</strong> – or another person <strong>voices their doubts</strong> – thus making you reconsider. Maybe the doubts don't even come from a fact, but you just have a <strong>sneaking suspicion</strong> – a persistent feeling – that <strong>casts doubts</strong> on things you previously assumed to be true.

When you have a small doubt that persists and continues to bother you – this is called a <strong>gnawing/lingering/nagging/niggling doubt</strong>. This might be an indication that it's time to <strong>rethink/reexamine your beliefs</strong> – after further consideration, you just might <strong>arrive at a different conclusion</strong>.
