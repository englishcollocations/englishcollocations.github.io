Lesson 26 – Time

Are you always <strong>in a rush</strong>, or do you <strong>take your time</strong>? Do you <strong>spend your time efficiently</strong>, or do you <strong>waste time</strong> and procrastinate important tasks – then stay up working until <strong>ungodly hours</strong> (very late at night/early in the morning)?

It has <strong>taken me ages</strong> (taken a very long time), but I've finally adopted some time-management habits:

• Plan things <strong>well in advance</strong> and don't <strong>underestimate the amount of time</strong> things will take – otherwise you'll end up being a slave to your <strong>grueling schedule</strong> (very intense schedule).

• Take care of <strong>time-consuming tasks</strong> (things that take a lot of time) on a day when you have no <strong>pressing commitments</strong> (urgent appointments or things to do).

• <strong>Establish a timeline</strong> for major projects, so that you don't <strong>leave things to the last minute</strong> and risk <strong>running out of time</strong>. Then, <strong>stick to the schedule</strong> and try to <strong>get ahead</strong> if possible. There's nothing worse than <strong>falling behind</strong> on a project with a <strong>strict deadline</strong> (a definite date before which it needs to be finished).

• Leave home and work a little earlier than normal to avoid <strong>rush hour</strong> (the time when there is a lot of traffic because everyone is going to work or coming home from work).

• Sleep for <strong>eight solid hours</strong> (eight hours without interruption) every night; you'll wake up with more energy.

If you're working <strong>from dawn till dusk</strong> (from the early morning to the late evening) and you have little or no <strong>spare</strong>/<strong>free time</strong>, it could mean that you're overworked, and you should <strong>cut back on your hours</strong> (reduce your working hours) to <strong>free up some time</strong> (make more time available).
