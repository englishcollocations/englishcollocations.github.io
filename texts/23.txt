Lesson 23 – Law/Justice

The government is responsible for <strong>passing laws</strong> (approving new laws to be put into practice), and the courts and legal system are in charge of <strong>upholding</strong> and <strong>enforcing the law</strong> (applying the law to the population).

When a person is <strong>charged with a crime</strong> (formally accused of the crime), he or she must <strong>appear in court</strong> to <strong>stand trial</strong> (be judged guilty or innocent). Both the <strong>prosecution</strong> and the <strong>defense</strong> will <strong>present their cases</strong> before the judge and jury. After consideration, the judge or jury will <strong>reach a verdict</strong> (arrive at a decision).

If there is <strong>irrefutable</strong>, <strong>overwhelming</strong>, or <strong>concrete evidence</strong>, this means that it is very obvious that the person committed the crime, and it's possible that the jury will be <strong>unanimous</strong> (meaning they all agree) in their decision. However, if the evidence is <strong>circumstantial</strong> or <strong>inconclusive</strong> (the evidence does not 100% prove the conclusion), then the decision will be more difficult – it's possible to have a <strong>hung jury</strong>, which cannot agree on the verdict.

Less serious crimes will receive a <strong>light sentence</strong> (a small punishment) – for example, the offender may have to <strong>pay a fine</strong> or do <strong>community service</strong>. For more serious crimes, the person will probably <strong>go to prison</strong> – and especially <strong>heinous crimes</strong> can result in a <strong>harsh sentence</strong> such as <strong>life in prison</strong> (or <strong>life imprisonment</strong>) or, in some places, the <strong>death penalty</strong>.

After the decision is made, the lawyers can choose to <strong>appeal the verdict</strong> (try to change the decision), and the case may turn into a <strong>prolonged legal battle</strong> as the courts decide whether to <strong>uphold</strong> or <strong>overturn the original verdict</strong> – "uphold" meaning to continue with the original decision, and "overturn" meaning to change the decision.

As a convicted criminal is <strong>serving time</strong> (spending time in prison), he or she should remember the possibility of being <strong>released from prison early</strong> for <strong>good behavior</strong>, as long as they are not deemed a <strong>threat to society</strong> (possibly dangerous to other people).

A criminal who is released early is said to be "<strong>on parole</strong>," meaning that he or she must <strong>comply with certain conditions</strong> after getting out of jail – such as getting a job and refraining from drug/alcohol use.
