Lesson 11 – Health & Sickness

There are a number of <strong>lifestyle changes</strong> you can make to improve your <strong>overall health</strong> and increase your <strong>life expectancy</strong> (how long you are expected to live) – you can <strong>quit smoking</strong>, <strong>reduce stress</strong>, <strong>eat a balanced diet</strong>, <strong>get plenty of sleep</strong>, and <strong>exercise regularly</strong>. It's also important to get <strong>vaccinations</strong>/<strong>immunizations</strong> to <strong>prevent diseases</strong>. No matter how healthy you are, it's still a good idea to have <strong>health insurance</strong> – a program/plan to cover expenses in case of an accident or serious illness.

If you're in a car accident, you might <strong>suffer</strong>/<strong>sustain serious injuries</strong>. Of course, if you're wearing your seatbelt, you're more likely to have only <strong>minor injuries</strong>. If you've <strong>broken</strong>/<strong>fractured a bone</strong> – for example, your leg – the doctor will <strong>put your leg in a cast</strong>. If you have a cut, the doctor will <strong>give you stitches</strong>. If you have a burn, the doctor will give you an <strong>ointment</strong> to <strong>prevent infection</strong>.

If you have a <strong>minor ailment</strong> like a headache, you can <strong>take some aspirin</strong> to <strong>relieve the pain</strong> (reduce the effects of the pain). If you <strong>suffer from a chronic condition</strong> (a condition that continues for a long time) like allergies, asthma, back pain, or depression, there are both <strong>over-the-counter</strong> and <strong>prescription medications</strong> that can help <strong>alleviate the symptoms</strong> (an over-the-counter medication is one that you can buy without a doctor's prescription).

When someone has a minor <strong>respiratory infection</strong>, we say they <strong>have a cold</strong>. Don't get too close to that person, because you could <strong>catch a cold</strong>, too! You can "catch" other types of <strong>contagious diseases</strong> as well – such as <strong>sexually-transmitted diseases</strong> (STDs).

If someone is <strong>diagnosed with</strong> a more serious illness such as cancer or AIDS, they'll need more <strong>extensive treatment</strong>. When a disease has progressed so far that it will be impossible to cure, then the patient is said to be <strong>terminally ill</strong> – in other words, the person will definitely die of the disease soon.
