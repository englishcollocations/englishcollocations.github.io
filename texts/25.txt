Lesson 25 – Global Problems

In today's lesson, we'll focus on several major global problems.

One critical issue is <strong>preserving and protecting the environment</strong>. The burning of <strong>fossil fuels</strong> (oil, coal, and natural gas) produces <strong>greenhouse gases</strong> (gases that heat up the atmosphere) that <strong>deplete the ozone layer</strong> and result in <strong>global warming</strong> and <strong>climate change</strong> (changes in the earth's temperature and weather patterns).

Some scientists believe that we have already done <strong>irreparable damage</strong> to the planet, and now we are suffering the consequences of <strong>rising sea levels</strong> and increasingly frequent <strong>natural disasters</strong> such as earthquakes, hurricanes, and tornados. A number of animal species have <strong>gone extinct</strong>, and many more are <strong>endangered</strong>. To reverse this trend, we must invest in <strong>clean energy</strong> (energy sources that do not pollute the environment) with <strong>renewable resources</strong> (elements like water and air, which can be re-used) and utilize manufacturing methods that are not <strong>harmful to the environment</strong>.

There are still many regions of the world where children <strong>die of hunger</strong>/<strong>starvation</strong> (die from not having enough food). Unfortunately, <strong>extreme poverty</strong> is common in the rural areas of <strong>developing countries</strong>, where the population often suffers from <strong>food shortages</strong> (not enough food) and <strong>lack of access to clean water</strong>.

In the cities, <strong>homeless people</strong> sleep on the streets if there is not enough room in local <strong>homeless shelters</strong>. There are also many families <strong>on the margins of society</strong> (outside the dominant society and culture) who are <strong>living below the poverty line</strong> and struggle to <strong>make ends meet</strong> every month. Although many governments create <strong>welfare programs</strong> (programs that give money to poor people) to <strong>alleviate poverty</strong>, the <strong>widening gap between rich and poor</strong> suggests that poverty may never be completely <strong>eradicated</strong>.

Poverty and underdevelopment contribute to a host of additional problems as well. For example, many people <strong>die of preventable diseases</strong> because they have no access the safe and effective vaccines that exist. A number of large corporations use <strong>child labor</strong> in the manufacturing of their products, as poor families may feel they have no other option for survival. In addition, young women from <strong>disadvantaged backgrounds</strong> (poor families and situations) are prime targets for <strong>human trafficking</strong> and <strong>sexual exploitation</strong>.
