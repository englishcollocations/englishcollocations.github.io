Lesson 10 – Sports

The first important collocation involving sports is when to use the verbs <strong>play</strong>, <strong>do</strong>, and <strong>go</strong>. We typically use <strong>play</strong> for team sports – you <strong>play soccer</strong>, <strong>play basketball</strong>, <strong>play tennis</strong>, <strong>play baseball</strong>, and <strong>play golf</strong>.

The verb <strong>do</strong> is used for more individual sports – you <strong>do yoga</strong>, <strong>do gymnastics</strong>, <strong>do martial arts</strong>, and <strong>do aerobics</strong>. Also, in more general terms, you <strong>do exercise</strong>. Another common individual exercise is to <strong>lift weights</strong>.

The verb <strong>go</strong> is used with most activities that end in –ing: you <strong>go swimming</strong>, <strong>go biking</strong>, <strong>go surfing</strong>, <strong>go rock climbing</strong>, <strong>go bowling</strong>, and <strong>go fishing</strong>.

With the team sports, we can use the word <strong>game</strong> or <strong>match</strong>: a <strong>soccer game</strong>, a <strong>basketball game</strong>, a <strong>tennis match</strong>, etc. You can <strong>win the game</strong>, <strong>lose the game</strong>, or <strong>tie the game</strong> – that's when the final score is 1-1 or 2-2, for example.

A team can play a <strong>home game</strong> – when they play in their own stadium or field – or an <strong>away game</strong> – when they play at the opposing team's stadium or field. When there are many teams that are playing many games to see which one is the best, we call this a <strong>tournament</strong>.

When one team is winning in the middle of the game – for example, 3-1 – we say that team <strong>has the lead</strong>. However, the other team can <strong>make a comeback</strong> – score points from a losing position – and <strong>take the lead</strong>, 4-3.

With the sports that use "do" and "go," we typically use the word <strong>competition</strong> – a <strong>gymnastics competition</strong>, a <strong>martial arts competition</strong>, a <strong>surfing competition</strong>, an <strong>ice skating competition</strong>, etc. The exception is sports where you go from one place to another – like running, biking, and swimming – in that case, we often call the event a <strong>race</strong>.

Athletes can <strong>enter a competition</strong> and try to <strong>win first place</strong>. Some athletes try to <strong>enhance their performance</strong> with illegal substances such as <strong>steroids</strong>. But if they <strong>fail a drug test</strong>, they'll be <strong>disqualified from participating</strong>.

If the athlete is performing well, they may <strong>achieve a personal best</strong> – and if they perform better than ANYONE ever has in the past, then they could even <strong>break the world record</strong> or <strong>set a new world record</strong>. If they <strong>get injured</strong>, however, they might have to <strong>withdraw from the competition</strong> (voluntarily leave the competition).
