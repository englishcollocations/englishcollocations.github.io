

Lesson 7 – Eating

My mother radically changed her <strong>eating habits</strong> a few years ago. In the past, she ate a lot of <strong>junk food</strong> (food that is not nutritious/healthy) and was overweight. Sometimes she would try to <strong>go on a diet</strong> (eat less food and healthier food), but it would never last. But now she understands how unhealthy <strong>processed foods</strong> are – they're full of <strong>food additives</strong> (chemicals added to food) that contribute to weight gain and heart problems.
Nowadays, she eats a <strong>balanced diet</strong> including a lot of <strong>nourishing meals</strong> (meals that are healthy and nutritious) that she prepares herself. She doesn't buy <strong>organic food</strong> because she thinks the prices are outrageous, but she <strong>stocks up on</strong> <strong>fresh produce</strong> (fresh fruits and vegetables) so that she can grab an apple or some veggies when she wants a <strong>quick snack</strong> (something small and fast to eat). She has also learned to <strong>eat in moderation</strong> and watch her <strong>portion sizes</strong> (how much food you take).
I often stop by her house when I'm in the mood for a <strong>home-cooked meal</strong>. The other day she made a <strong>hearty stew</strong> (a thick soup with lots of flavors and ingredients) that was full of beans and vegetables. My brother, who has a <strong>healthy appetite</strong> (the capacity to eat a lot), had a <strong>second helping</strong> (a second portion) – but I found the stew so <strong>filling</strong> that I could barely finish my bowl.
My mother recently told me that she'd like to try some <strong>international cuisine</strong>. I was surprised to hear that, because a few years ago she had a bad experience after getting <strong>food poisoning</strong> (when you get sick because of contaminated food) at a Japanese restaurant. So tomorrow I'm taking her to a Brazilian steakhouse. I told her to eat <strong>light meals</strong> (small meals) during the day so that she doesn't <strong>spoil her appetite</strong> for dinner ("spoiling your appetite" means eating food earlier, and then not being hungry for a meal later).
