Lesson 29 – Light

We use the expression "<strong>the sun is shining</strong>" to talk about the sun emitting light. If the light is so strong that it's difficult to see, we can describe it as <strong>blinding sunlight</strong> – and if the light is not very strong, then it is <strong>weak sunlight</strong>.

When <strong>the sun sets</strong> (disappears below the horizon), everything grows dark. If there's a <strong>full moon</strong>, then there will still be some light. But if there's a <strong>crescent moon</strong> or a <strong>new moon</strong>, then it will be <strong>pitch dark/black</strong> (completely black) and you'll be able to see the <strong>stars twinkling</strong> in the night sky.

To describe the strength of a light, you can use the words "bright" and "dim." A fluorescent light bulb emits <strong>bright light</strong>, and the faint glow of a flickering candle is <strong>dim light</strong>. When light shines on an object, it makes the object <strong>cast a shadow</strong>. An area that is protected from the sunlight is often called "<strong>the shade</strong>." If you don't want to get sunburned, it's a good idea to <strong>sit in the shade</strong>.

We use the word "lighting" to refer to a system of artificial lights, especially inside a house or building. A place can have <strong>bright/strong lighting</strong> or the opposite – <strong>soft/subdued lighting</strong> (a low level of illumination for artistic/romantic purposes). And along the highways and roads, we have <strong>street lights</strong>.

If it's nighttime and <strong>the power goes out</strong> (or there is a <strong>power outage</strong> – a lack of electrical power), then the city will be <strong>plunged into darkness</strong>. You can describe the darkness as <strong>complete/total darkness</strong> to emphasize that it is 100% dark. People will have to use <strong>flashlights</strong> or <strong>light candles</strong> to be able to see – until the next morning when <strong>the sun rises/comes up</strong>.
