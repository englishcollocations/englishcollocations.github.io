Lesson 5 – Feelings

I experienced a <strong>roller coaster of emotions</strong> when my son was born. During my wife’s pregnancy, I was <strong>ridiculously excited</strong> about the prospect of becoming a dad. On the big day, I’d imagined that everything would <strong>go smoothly</strong> and we’d be <strong>blissfully happy</strong> – but there were complications during the delivery and my wife had to have <strong>emergency surgery</strong> while I waited in the reception area, <strong>worried sick</strong>.
As time went by, I got <strong>increasingly anxious</strong> because the doctors weren’t telling me anything. After two hours I was <strong>seething with anger</strong>, and I shouted at one of the nurses. Then the security guards came and escorted me out of the hospital.
My wife was <strong>visibly disappointed</strong> that I wasn’t by her side when she woke up from surgery. She didn’t say anything – she’s not really one to <strong>show her feelings</strong>; she prefers to <strong>bottle up her emotions</strong> – but I knew I had <strong>let her down</strong> and I was terribly sorry that I had <strong>lost my temper</strong>.
The months that followed were tough. My wife became <strong>deeply depressed</strong>, and I too was an <strong>emotional wreck</strong> as I tried to <strong>juggle the demands</strong> of work and family life. But now things are starting to <strong>turn around</strong> – I’ve been <strong>pleasantly surprised</strong> at all the help we’ve gotten from our friends and neighbors.
I’m just <strong>immensely grateful</strong> that my wife and son are healthy, and sometimes I’m <strong>overwhelmed with emotion</strong> as I reflect on how lucky we are.
