Lesson 41 – Memories

I'm trying to think of my <strong>earliest memory</strong>. I <strong>vaguely remember</strong> (remember a little bit, not in much detail) when my brother was born – I was three years old at the time – but I <strong>distinctly remember</strong> my first day of kindergarten, when I was around five.

When I look at photos, even more memories <strong>come flooding back</strong> (memories come to mind) – I remember my school, my classmates, and my teacher, who would constantly remind us to clean up the classroom, because we were always making a mess. I'd <strong>completely forgotten</strong> the teacher's name, though, and my mother had to <strong>refresh my memory</strong> (remind me of a fact I'd forgotten).

I still have a lot of my toys from when I was a kid. They <strong>bring back fond memories</strong> (pleasant memories) of long afternoons spent playing with my brother. I also remember a family vacation to Switzerland when I was about eight – that was an <strong>unforgettable experience</strong>.

My most <strong>traumatic memory</strong> is of my uncle dying in a car crash when I was ten. He was only 32 years old – younger than my dad – and the memory of the funeral still <strong>haunts me</strong> (the sad/profound memory stays with me). I still wear a bracelet that was a Christmas present from him; it <strong>serves as a sobering reminder</strong> (it provides a serious reminder) not to take a single day of life for granted.

Unfortunately, I have a <strong>terrible memory for peoples' names</strong>, unless the name is unusual or particularly memorable. Just the other day, I was talking to an acquaintance from church and my <strong>mind went blank</strong> (I couldn't think of anything) when I tried to think of her name. I <strong>racked my brain</strong> (tried hard to remember something), but nothing <strong>came to mind</strong>. My husband gently reminds me that I really need to pay more attention, because it makes a bad impression when I call people by the wrong name.
