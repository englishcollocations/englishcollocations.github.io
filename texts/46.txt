Lesson 46 – Liking & Disliking

“I’m a <strong>lifelong fan</strong> of U2 – they’re my <strong>all-time favorite</strong> band. In addition to the <strong>sheer enjoyment</strong> (pure pleasure) of listening to their music, I’m also a <strong>great admirer</strong> of Bono and his philanthropic work. He’s concerned not only with the <strong>fleeting pleasure</strong> (momentary enjoyment) of fame and fortune, but also with making a difference in the world. I <strong>hold him in the highest regard</strong> (have great respect for him) for this reason.”

“When my daughter was a baby, I noticed that she developed a <strong>particular liking</strong> for classical music, and when she was six I signed her up for violin classes. It was a <strong>real pleasure</strong> to see her <strong>obvious enjoyment</strong> of the lessons. She <strong>grew quite attached</strong> (strongly like/love over time) to her teacher, who says that she has the potential to turn her <strong>great love</strong> of music into a professional career. Now my daughter is 13 and lately she’s expressed a <strong>clear preference</strong> for the most difficult pieces of music – she says she wants a challenge.”

“I took an <strong>instant dislike</strong> to Sam from the moment we were introduced. Within five minutes, he was talking about his <strong>deep dislike</strong> of foreigners – without knowing that my own parents were immigrants from Argentina. He said he had <strong>nothing but contempt</strong> (only hate) for people who abandoned their own countries to come to ours, and then began making horribly racist jokes and comments. I have no idea where Sam’s <strong>blind hatred</strong> / <strong>irrational hatred</strong> (hatred without a logical reason) of foreigners comes from, but he seems to have developed a <strong>strong aversion</strong> (developed an intense dislike) to interacting with anyone who’s the slightest bit different from him. I try to be polite, but deep down I <strong>hate him with a passion</strong> (hate him a LOT).”
