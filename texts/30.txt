Lesson 30 – Color

Today you're going to expand your vocabulary by learning various collocations about colors.

You can describe a color as <strong>light</strong> or <strong>dark</strong> – for example, someone can have <strong>light brown</strong> or <strong>dark brown hair</strong>. If a color is very intense, you can describe it as <strong>bright</strong> – for example, a <strong>bright red apple</strong>. If the color is not very intense, you can describe it as <strong>pale</strong> – for example, a <strong>pale green dress</strong>.

When describing colors in general, you can talk about <strong>bold/vivid colors</strong> – which are very intense – or <strong>pastel colors</strong>, which are light or pale.

We can use other words to describe specific <strong>shades of a color</strong> (variations of a specific color). These words are usually a typical object with that particular color. For example, "<strong>sky blue</strong>" is a light shade of blue, like the color of the sky. Here are some of the most common collocations:

<strong>ruby red</strong>, <strong>cherry red</strong>, <strong>fiery orange</strong>, <strong>burnt orange</strong>, <strong>lemon yellow</strong>, <strong>golden yellow</strong>, <strong>emerald green</strong>, <strong>olive green</strong>, <strong>sky blue</strong>, <strong>royal blue</strong>, <strong>navy blue</strong> / <strong>midnight blue</strong>

When you have something that is primarily one color, but with a little bit of another color included, we can use the word "<strong>tinge</strong>." There are two ways to structure the sentence:

• The flowers are yellow <strong>with a tinge of pink</strong> in the middle.
• My cat's fur is light gray <strong>with a slight brownish tinge</strong>.

You can also use this form – a color word plus <strong>–ISH</strong> – to describe mixes of colors. For example, "<strong>reddish brown</strong>" is a shade of brown with a strong portion of red, whereas "<strong>yellowish brown</strong>" is a shade of brown with a strong portion of yellow.

Some colors look good together – in this case, we can say that the <strong>colors match</strong>. Other colors look terrible together – in this case, we can say that the <strong>colors clash</strong>. When the color of a person's clothes looks good with that person's skin tone (color of their skin), you can say "<strong>that color suits you</strong>" or "<strong>that color looks good on you</strong>."

Finally, one tip about changing the color of something. We use the verb "<strong>paint</strong>" in most cases – you <strong>paint a house</strong> and <strong>paint your nails</strong>. But we use the verb "<strong>dye</strong>" for hair and cloth/fabric – so you <strong>dye your hair blonde</strong> and <strong>dye a shirt blue</strong>.
