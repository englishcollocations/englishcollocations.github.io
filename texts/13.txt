Lesson 13 – Study

Most children <strong>begin their studies</strong> at 5 or 6 years old in <strong>pre-school</strong>, followed by <strong>kindergarten</strong>. After that comes <strong>primary school</strong>/<strong>elementary school</strong>, which consists of <strong>first grade</strong> through <strong>fifth grade</strong>. Then there's <strong>middle school</strong> – <strong>sixth grade</strong> through <strong>eighth grade</strong> – and <strong>high school</strong>, which is <strong>ninth grade</strong> through <strong>twelfth grade</strong>.

After high school, you <strong>apply to colleges</strong>/<strong>universities</strong> to see which ones will accept you. You <strong>send in</strong>/<strong>submit your application</strong> and then wait for the results. If you <strong>got good grades</strong> in high school, you might <strong>get</strong> / <strong>be awarded a scholarship</strong> to help pay the <strong>tuition</strong> (the cost of studying).

In college, you'll <strong>take classes</strong>/<strong>courses</strong> on various subjects. You should always <strong>take notes</strong> as the teacher is <strong>giving the lecture</strong>. Some teachers may ask the students to <strong>give</strong>/<strong>make presentations</strong> or <strong>do research</strong> and <strong>write an essay</strong>, an <strong>assignment</strong>, a <strong>report</strong>, or a <strong>paper</strong>. After you <strong>hand in your work</strong>, the teacher will <strong>grade the assignment</strong> (give you an evaluation, for example, 90%) and <strong>give you feedback</strong> (comments on your work).

To evaluate your progress, you'll <strong>take tests</strong>/<strong>exams</strong>. If you get the minimum number of points required, you've <strong>passed the test</strong>; if you don't get the minimum number of points, you've <strong>failed the test</strong>. It's also possible to <strong>pass</strong>/<strong>fail a course</strong>.

If the course proves to be too difficult, or if you don't like it, you can <strong>withdraw from</strong> or <strong>drop the course</strong>. "Withdraw from" is the more formal expression, and "drop" is the more informal expression.

Most college students <strong>choose a major</strong>/<strong>concentration</strong>. To talk about a concentration of studies, you can use "major" as a verb or a noun – for example,
"I <strong>majored in biology</strong>" / "I was a <strong>biology major</strong>."
"My daughter is <strong>majoring in philosophy</strong>." / "My daughter's a <strong>philosophy major</strong>."

When you <strong>graduate from college</strong>, you'll <strong>get a diploma</strong>. Afterwards, you can say you <strong>have a degree</strong> in the area of your concentration. You can have a <strong>bachelor's degree</strong>, <strong>master's degree</strong>, or <strong>doctorate</strong> (Ph.D.), for example:
"I have a <strong>bachelor's degree in chemistry</strong>."
"I have a <strong>master's degree in linguistics</strong>."
"I have a <strong>Ph.D. in theology</strong>."
