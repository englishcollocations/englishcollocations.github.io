Lesson 4 – Character & Behavior

When I was a teenager, I was <strong>painfully shy</strong> (extremely shy). I loved reading books, and I had a <strong>vivid imagination</strong> (a creative and colorful imagination). My best friend had a more <strong>outgoing personality</strong> (liked to be friendly and sociable) and a <strong>good sense of humor</strong> (ability to make other people laugh). Unfortunately, she was also <strong>brutally honest</strong>, and sometimes her comments <strong>hurt my feelings</strong> (made me sad, upset, or angry).
Although I was <strong>fiercely loyal</strong> to her, she <strong>showed her true colors</strong> (revealed her true personality) when she <strong>played a prank</strong> (did a trick) that humiliated me in front of the class. I <strong>bore a grudge</strong> (continued feeling angry) against her for years afterwards. Now that I look back on it, I can see that she had <strong>low self-esteem</strong> (considered herself inferior) and a bit of a <strong>mean streak</strong> (a “streak” is a tiny bit of someone’s personality).
I've <strong>come out of my shell</strong> (become less shy) since those days; I have a <strong>thicker skin</strong> (I’m less sensitive to criticism) and it doesn't bother me when people <strong>speak their minds</strong> (say exactly what they think, without considering the effects of the words on others).
My biggest flaw is that I have a tendency to be arrogant – some say I have a <strong>superiority complex</strong> (consider myself superior to others). I’ll admit that it is hard for me to <strong>swallow my pride</strong> (stop being arrogant) and recognize when I've been mistaken about something. My resolution for the New Year is to make a more <strong>concerted effort</strong> to <strong>put others first</strong> (consider the needs of other people more important than my own).
