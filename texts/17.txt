Lesson 17 – Money

There are a lot of things you can do with money!

First, you need to <strong>earn money</strong> or <strong>make money</strong> – both of these verbs refer to receiving money from your work. Another way to obtain money is to <strong>inherit money</strong> – receive money from a family member or friend after that person dies. If the person was very rich, you could <strong>inherit a fortune</strong> (a fortune is a lot of money).

You can take your money to the bank and <strong>make a deposit</strong> – put money into your bank account. Later, you can <strong>make a withdrawal</strong> – remove money from your bank account. Of course, everyone likes to <strong>spend money</strong> – use money to buy things – but it's important not to <strong>waste</strong>/<strong>squander your money</strong>. These verbs refer to using money in a way that's not productive.

If <strong>money is tight</strong> (meaning you have very little money), you should <strong>make a budget</strong> – a plan for how much to spend on things every month – and then you need to <strong>stick to the budget</strong> (continue following the plan). Otherwise, you might be <strong>broke</strong> by the end of the month! Someone who is "broke" has no money available.

A person who is <strong>financially savvy</strong> (intelligent, perceptive, and well-informed on the subject of money) will always <strong>save money</strong>. It's a good idea to have an <strong>emergency fund</strong> of at least 3-6 months of <strong>living expenses</strong> (the amount money you use to survive in everyday life). You can also <strong>invest money</strong> in the <strong>stock market</strong> – you could <strong>make a nice profit</strong>, but there's also the risk of <strong>losing money</strong> if the economy <strong>goes downhill</strong> (starts to get worse).

Let's say you want to purchase an expensive item like a car. If you haven't <strong>saved up money</strong> or <strong>set aside money</strong> for the purchase, you can <strong>borrow money</strong> from the bank – which is also called <strong>taking out a loan</strong>. The disadvantage of this is that you'll need to <strong>pay the money back</strong> in <strong>installments</strong> – plus <strong>interest</strong> (extra money).

When you travel, you'll need to <strong>change</strong>/<strong>exchange money</strong> to be able to buy things with the <strong>local currency</strong>. Make sure to <strong>change money</strong> only at official locations like banks and travel agencies – otherwise, you could be tricked into receiving <strong>counterfeit bills</strong> (false paper money that is not legally valid).

Finally, you can consider <strong>donating money</strong> (giving money without receiving anything in return) to a good cause, such as preserving the environment or researching cures for diseases. Non-profit organizations are always trying to <strong>raise money</strong> (get donations) for their various projects.
