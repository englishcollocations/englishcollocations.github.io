Lesson 14 – Academic English

Before you begin writing a paper, it's a good idea to <strong>make an outline</strong> (a list of points in an organized order) of the main points you plan to present. The next step is to <strong>gather evidence</strong> to <strong>support your claims</strong>, since your work won't be credible if you <strong>propose a theory</strong> (or try to <strong>challenge</strong> or <strong>refute a theory</strong> – argue against an existing theory) without some proof.

If your paper is going to <strong>touch on several issues</strong> (talk about several topics) then you'll need to be especially organized. You can <strong>draw a distinction</strong> between topics that are different, or <strong>draw parallels</strong> between examples that are similar. Another way to organize your ideas is to show how they <strong>fall into different categories</strong>.

After <strong>making your outline</strong> and gathering the <strong>supporting evidence</strong> you'll use to <strong>make your case</strong> (present your argument), you can write a <strong>first draft</strong> (first version of the paper) in which you <strong>go into detail</strong> on the topic. Then, you <strong>revise the paper</strong> (make improvements to it) until finishing with the <strong>final draft</strong>.

Let's say you're <strong>carrying out a study</strong> on market trends in developing countries. You can <strong>analyze the key factors</strong> (important factors) that are influencing the economy, showing how local politics <strong>play a role</strong>/<strong>part</strong> (have an effect) in <strong>shaping the country's financial future</strong>. Including some specific items that are <strong>perfect examples</strong> and <strong>clear illustrations</strong> of your ideas will help <strong>prove your points</strong>.

At the end of your paper, you should briefly <strong>summarize the material</strong> you presented and <strong>draw conclusions</strong> based on your research. The end of the article is also a good place to <strong>raise questions</strong> (present questions) for <strong>further study</strong>.
