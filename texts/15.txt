Lesson 15 – Work

If you've just graduated from college and you've never had a job, you can <strong>start your career</strong> by doing an <strong>internship</strong> (a temporary, low-level job) to <strong>gain experience</strong>. Depending on the company, the internship could lead to an opportunity to get an <strong>entry-level job</strong> (a job that doesn't require much skill or experience) at the same company.

Although you might be <strong>earning minimum wage</strong> (the minimum salary required by law), if you <strong>do excellent work</strong> and are a good <strong>team player</strong> (you cooperate well with your co-workers) you could <strong>get a promotion</strong> (get an increase in responsibility) or <strong>get a raise</strong> (get an increase in salary) – hopefully both! Just don't <strong>slack off</strong>, otherwise you'll be <strong>fired</strong>/<strong>dismissed</strong> (lose your job).

It's normal for there to be periods of time when you have a <strong>heavy workload</strong> (a lot of work) and need to <strong>work overtime</strong> (work extra hours). But if you have a very <strong>demanding job</strong> (an intense job with lots of responsibilities) and you're <strong>stressed out</strong> all the time, then it might be time to <strong>quit your job</strong> (leave your job voluntarily) and look for another one.

Now that you have experience, you'll be able to get a job with a more <strong>competitive salary</strong> (salary that is higher than average) and <strong>generous benefits</strong> (extra benefits like health insurance, discounts, a company car, etc). If you can't find a <strong>well-paid</strong> and <strong>rewarding job</strong> in your field, it might be time for a <strong>career change</strong>.

The typical way of <strong>applying for a job</strong> is to read the <strong>job ads</strong> in the newspaper or online, then <strong>send your resume</strong> to the company with a <strong>cover letter</strong>. The company will then <strong>interview the candidates</strong> it feels are most qualified, based on the <strong>job description</strong>.

Sometimes <strong>job seekers</strong> (people looking for a job) need to take a <strong>menial job</strong> (job requiring a very low level of skill) in order to <strong>earn a living</strong> (get enough money to survive) while they're <strong>between jobs</strong>. But if you're persistent, a great <strong>job offer</strong> should come your way sooner or later – maybe even for your <strong>dream job</strong> (a job that would be perfect for you)!
