Lesson 34 – Statistics

First, let's clarify the difference between "number" and "amount" – we use "number" with countable nouns, and "amount" with uncountable nouns.

• A <strong>large number of people</strong>
• A <strong>massive amount of damage</strong>
• A <strong>significant number of countries</strong>
• A <strong>considerable amount of money</strong>
• A <strong>tiny number of computers</strong>
• A <strong>minuscule amount of alcohol</strong>

Numbers and statistics can <strong>increase</strong> or <strong>decrease</strong> (more formal), <strong>rise</strong> or <strong>fall</strong> (also formal) or <strong>go up</strong> and <strong>go down</strong> (more informally). When a number goes up or down very fast, we can describe this as <strong>sharp</strong> or <strong>dramatic</strong> – for example, the sudden improvement in the economy has resulted in a <strong>dramatic drop</strong> in unemployment and a <strong>sharp rise</strong> in consumer spending. When a number goes up or down slowly, then we can talk about a <strong>gradual increase</strong> in population or a <strong>slow decline</strong> in the number of violent crimes.

And if a number doesn't change, then it <strong>remains steady</strong> or <strong>remains constant</strong>.

We use the word "<strong>widespread</strong>" to describe something that is happening frequently in many places. For example, there being <strong>widespread dissatisfaction</strong> with the government, or a new book winning <strong>widespread acclaim</strong> in the literary world. A hurricane can cause <strong>widespread damage</strong>, and a presidential candidate can have <strong>widespread support</strong> among voters of a certain demographic.

In conversational English, we often use the structure <strong>keep + -ING</strong> to describe something that happens frequently. For example,

"I'm trying to work, but my colleagues <strong>keep interrupting</strong> me."
"Why do you <strong>keep asking</strong> me about the problem? I've told you a thousand times I don't know how to solve it."
