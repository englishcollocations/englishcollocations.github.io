

Lesson 8 – Movies / Books


My brother is a huge fan of movies. When he sees a <strong>movie trailer</strong> (a short video offering a preview of the movie), he counts down the days until the movie <strong>comes out</strong>. He always goes to the movie theater on <strong>opening night</strong> – that's the first day a movie, show, concert, or performance is open to the public. He also loves to analyze the <strong>film critics' reviews</strong> (the evaluations of the people whose job is to analyze movies) and tries to guess which movies will be <strong>nominated for an award</strong>.
One of his favorite <strong>movie stars</strong> (famous actors/actresses) will be <strong>starring in</strong> an <strong>upcoming film</strong> about the Vietnam War. It's <strong>based on a true story</strong>, and they're <strong>shooting the film</strong> <strong>on-site</strong> in Vietnam – that means they will be recording/filming the movie in the same location as the story occurs.
This particular actor had been talking about retiring, but when he was offered the <strong>leading role</strong> (the opportunity to play the most important part in a movie) in a film that's sure to be a <strong>box-office hit</strong> (a movie that is very successful) he couldn't <strong>turn it down</strong>.
I, on the other hand, prefer books. I'm currently <strong>engrossed in a book</strong> by Stieg Larsson (the book is taking all my attention) – it's the final part of a trilogy, and I can't wait to see how things turn out for the <strong>main character</strong> (the most important person in the book). I already have a huge <strong>collection of books</strong>, and I also <strong>borrow books from the library</strong>.
One book I <strong>took out</strong> last week <strong>deals with</strong> modern feminism and motherhood. It's a bit of a <strong>controversial topic</strong>, but it's an interesting and <strong>enjoyable read</strong>. I'm not sure I'll have time to <strong>finish it</strong> before I have to <strong>return the book</strong> to the library. Maybe I'll <strong>renew it</strong> for another week – that means to get an extension of the length of time I can keep the book.
Note: We talk about the <strong>beginning and end</strong> of a book, story, or film (not the "start and finish"). The first and last parts of a story can also be called the <strong>opening and closing scenes</strong> (in a movie) or the <strong>opening and closing chapters</strong> (in a book).
