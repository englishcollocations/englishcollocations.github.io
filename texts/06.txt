Lesson 6 – Houses and Apartments

I recently spent a month on vacation – but instead of staying in a hotel, I looked for a <strong>short-term rental</strong> (a place to live that can be rented for a short period of time). I ended up sharing a <strong>two-bedroom apartment</strong> – that’s an apartment with two bedrooms, a living room, and a kitchen – with some friends. It was actually cheaper than renting a <strong>studio apartment</strong> (an apartment with only one big room) all by myself.
The apartment was on the <strong>top floor</strong> of a <strong>five-story building</strong> (that’s a building with five floors) and it had a <strong>balcony overlooking</strong> the ocean (that means you can see the ocean from the balcony). From the roof, we also had a nice view of the <strong>city skyline</strong> (this is another expression used when you can see something from a certain place).
The apartment had <strong>recently been remodeled</strong>/<strong>renovated</strong> – meaning it was fixed up and improved – and was <strong>fully furnished</strong>, so it already had furniture (beds, tables, etc.) and appliances (refrigerator, stove, etc.)
We loved the <strong>spacious living room</strong> (the room was big), but unfortunately the bedrooms were <strong>rather cramped</strong> (small and uncomfortable). It was nice to spend a month there, but by the end of the trip I started to <strong>feel homesick</strong> – that’s when you feel sad because you miss your life at home.
I’d really like to have a <strong>place of my own</strong> so that I’m not <strong>throwing away money</strong> on rent every month, but there’s a real shortage of <strong>affordable housing</strong> (houses/apartments that are reasonably priced) in my city. Even if I stay away from the <strong>upscale neighborhoods</strong> (areas where rich people live), I’d still have to <strong>take out a mortgage</strong> (borrow money from the bank, in order to buy a house) and it’ll take me more than 30 years to <strong>pay it off</strong>.
But still, I’m gonna start <strong>saving up for</strong> a <strong>down payment</strong> (an initial payment on a big purchase). Someday, when I do <strong>move into</strong> a new place, I’ll be sure to throw a <strong>housewarming party</strong> (a party to celebrate living in a new house/apartment).
