Lesson 22 – Crime

When a person <strong>breaks the law</strong> (does something illegal), we say they have <strong>committed a crime</strong>. Especially horrible crimes – like a <strong>savage rape</strong> or a <strong>brutal murder</strong> – can be described as <strong>barbaric</strong>, <strong>horrible</strong>, or <strong>vicious crimes</strong>.

If someone tries to commit a crime but does not succeed, we use the word <strong>attempted</strong> – for example, an <strong>attempted murder</strong>, <strong>attempted kidnapping</strong>, or <strong>attempted break-in</strong>. If someone accuses another person of a crime, but it is not yet proven, then we use the word <strong>alleged</strong> – for example, an <strong>alleged assault</strong>, <strong>alleged harassment</strong>, or <strong>alleged rape</strong>.

After the victim <strong>reports the crime</strong> to the police, the police will begin to <strong>investigate the crime</strong>. They'll visit the <strong>crime scene</strong> and <strong>gather forensic evidence</strong> (collect details like bullets, fingerprints, or blood samples that can show what happened). If <strong>witnesses come forward</strong>, the police will <strong>interview them</strong> to hear a <strong>first-hand account</strong> (a story told by a person who saw the event) of what happened. They also consider possible <strong>motives for the crime</strong>.

Police can <strong>arrest suspects</strong> who they believe have committed a crime – but the suspect can <strong>prove their innocence</strong> by providing a <strong>solid alibi</strong> – that means giving a confirmed explanation of where they were at the time of the crime. If it is never discovered who committed the crime, then the case remains an <strong>unsolved crime</strong>. The statistics about number of crimes in a particular area is called the <strong>crime rate</strong>. And when there is a big increase in the crime rate, we can call this a <strong>crime wave</strong>.

There are also a number of collocations we can use to describe criminals. A <strong>first-time offender</strong> is someone who has broken the law for the first time, whereas someone who has broken the law in the past has a <strong>criminal record</strong>. A criminal who is famous – like a <strong>serial killer</strong> (a person who has killed multiple people over time) or a person who is heavily involved in <strong>organized crime</strong> – can be called a <strong>notorious criminal</strong>.

Collocations for specific crimes / criminals:
• <strong>cold-blooded killer</strong>/<strong>murderer</strong> – a person who doesn't have feeling or emotion
• <strong>crazed</strong>/<strong>psychopathic killer</strong> – a killer who is insane or mentally unbalanced
• <strong>domestic violence</strong> – when there is violence inside a home, for example, a husband beating his wife
• <strong>drug trafficking</strong> – the purchase, sale, and transport of illegal drugs
• <strong>identity theft</strong> – when a criminal steals an innocent person's identifying information and uses it in an illegal way
• <strong>sexual harassment</strong> – when one person makes undesired sexual advances towards another person
• <strong>petty crime</strong> – a minor crime
• <strong>armed robbery</strong> – when criminals steal something, while using weapons
