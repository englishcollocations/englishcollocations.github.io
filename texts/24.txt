Lesson 24 – War & Peace

When a war begins, we can say that <strong>war has broken out</strong> or <strong>violence has erupted</strong> in the region. When one country officially enters into a state of war, then they have <strong>declared war</strong> on the enemy country.

Sometimes, one country performs a <strong>pre-emptive strike</strong> (an early attack) against another – for example, if it suspects that the other country is <strong>stockpiling weapons</strong> (creating a collection of weapons) that will be used for a future attack. The other country may view this as an <strong>unprovoked attack</strong> (an unfair attack without a reason), and tensions may rise until the area is <strong>on the brink of war</strong> – meaning that war is very probable.

<strong>Sending in the military</strong> is often called <strong>deploying troops</strong>. The soldiers may be <strong>stationed</strong>/<strong>posted</strong> in a certain area to protect it, or they may <strong>launch an offensive</strong> in which they <strong>storm</strong>/<strong>invade enemy territory</strong>. When the soldiers begin firing their guns, we say that they <strong>opened fire</strong>.

Unfortunately, the <strong>horrors of war</strong> affect everyone, as innocent people are always <strong>caught in the crossfire</strong> (in the middle of the violence without participating in it). Both sides are sure to suffer <strong>civilian casualties</strong> (when innocent people are injured or killed) and there will be <strong>collateral damage</strong> to the countries' infrastructure. War also results in many <strong>refugees displaced by the violence</strong>; they may <strong>resettle</strong> in another area or <strong>flee to another country</strong>.

If a particular battle was an important turning point in the war, then it is called a <strong>decisive battle</strong>. Sometimes another country <strong>intervenes</strong> and helps the two sides <strong>negotiate a truce</strong>/<strong>ceasefire</strong> (agree to stop fighting). If successful, this can <strong>restore peace</strong> – but it may be a <strong>fragile</strong>/<strong>uneasy peace</strong>. More work will still be needed to <strong>bring stability</strong> to the area and <strong>establish a lasting peace</strong>. Often, <strong>peacekeeping forces</strong> remain in the region to try to <strong>avert war</strong> (avoid war).
