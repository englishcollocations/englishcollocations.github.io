Lesson 50 – Everyday Verbs

To finish up our study of collocations, we're going to focus on some expressions with everyday verbs. A lot of learners make mistakes with these verbs because the collocations are often different in their native languages.

**HAVE**

<strong>have a baby</strong> / <strong>have children</strong> (give birth to / raise children)
<strong>have a good time</strong> / <strong>have a blast</strong> / <strong>have fun</strong> (enjoy yourself)
<strong>have breakfast/lunch/dinner</strong> / <strong>a snack</strong> (eat meals)
<strong>have an opportunity</strong> (get a chance)
<strong>have an advantage</strong> (possess a benefit)

**TAKE**

<strong>take a shower/bath</strong> (wash yourself)
<strong>take a class/test/exam</strong> (attend class or do a test)
<strong>take a bus/train/taxi</strong> (travel by public transport)
<strong>take a break</strong> / <strong>take a rest</strong> (pause from an activity in progress)
<strong>take advantage of</strong> (use an opportunity)
<strong>take care of</strong> (look after someone/something)
<strong>take action</strong> (do something about a situation)

**PAY**

In the previous lesson, you learned collocations with pay involving money. Here are some collocations with pay that have nothing to do with money:
<strong>pay attention</strong> (give attention, observe)
<strong>pay tribute</strong> (give honor or recognition)
<strong>pay someone a compliment</strong> (give someone a compliment)
<strong>pay someone a visit</strong> (an informal way to say "visit someone")
<strong>pay (your) respects</strong> (express your admiration for someone, often used for someone who has died)

**BREAK**

<strong>break a habit</strong> (stop doing a habit)
<strong>break a promise</strong> (fail to do what you promised to do)
<strong>break the ice</strong> (informal: initiate conversation and social interaction, especially in a group that is quiet or reserved)
<strong>break the rules/law</strong> (disobey)
<strong>break the news to someone</strong> (tell someone important news, usually bad news)

**CATCH**

<strong>catch a cold</strong> / <strong>the flu</strong> (when you get sick due to a bacteria/virus transmitted from another person)
<strong>catch fire</strong> (when something begins to burn)
<strong>catch sight of</strong> / <strong>catch a glimpse of</strong> (get the opportunity to look at something)
<strong>catch someone's eye</strong> (make eye contact with someone)
<strong>catch the bus/train/subway</strong> (an informal way to say that you went on the bus, train, or subway)

**COME**

<strong>come into force/effect</strong> (begin to have an effect)
<strong>come to an end</strong> (finish)
<strong>come to terms with</strong> (learn to accept someone or something)
<strong>come under attack/scrutiny</strong> (be criticized or examined)
<strong>come close</strong> (to approximate someone or something in a specific quality)

**GET**

As you learned in Lesson 36 about changes, get can be used in the sense of "become":
<strong>get married/divorced</strong> (become married/divorced)
<strong>get drunk/pregnant</strong> (become drunk/pregnant)
<strong>get angry/scared/excited/tired</strong> (become angry/scared/excited/tired)
