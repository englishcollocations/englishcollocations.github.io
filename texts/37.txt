Lesson 37 – Speaking

Want to become a better conversationalist? Here are a few tips for making <strong>small talk</strong> (talking casually about simple topics):

• To <strong>strike up a conversation</strong> (start a conversation), you can make comments about the weather or the local surroundings.

• Stay away from <strong>delicate subjects</strong> (sensitive topics) like religion and politics – you don't want to get into a <strong>lengthy discussion</strong> (a long discussion) or <strong>have an argument</strong> (have a verbal fight).

• If there's a <strong>lull in the conversation</strong> (a pause in the conversation) ask the other person a question about themselves.

• If you tell a story, don't <strong>go on and on</strong> (talk too much) - remember that you're having a <strong>brief chat</strong> (a quick conversation); you're not making a speech.

• Be careful about <strong>cracking jokes</strong> – you might end up having to <strong>apologize profusely</strong> (apologize many times) if the person is offended.

Having good <strong>communication skills</strong> can really improve your relationships. People appreciate it when you <strong>get straight to the point</strong> (talk directly about the essential message) rather than <strong>dropping hints</strong> (trying to communicate very indirectly).

Of course, nobody trusts a person who <strong>tells lies</strong> (says things that aren't true) or <strong>stretches the truth</strong> (exaggerates and distorts the facts). If you always <strong>tell the truth</strong>, then people know they can count on you when you <strong>give your word</strong> (make a promise or statement of the truth).

It's also important to <strong>watch your tone of voice</strong> (the emotion with which you speak) and avoid making <strong>sarcastic remarks</strong> or <strong>disparaging comments</strong> (very negative and destructive comments). If you must make a complaint, try to give <strong>constructive criticism</strong> (criticism that helps the situation improve). And if you need to <strong>ask a favor</strong> of someone, always do it politely – don't forget to say "Please"!
