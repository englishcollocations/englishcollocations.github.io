Lesson 42 – Agreeing & Disagreeing

When you agree with someone completely, you can say "<strong>I totally agree</strong>" or "<strong>I strongly agree</strong>." Another way to describe 100% agreement is to say a person <strong>agrees wholeheartedly</strong>. On the other hand, you can say "<strong>I agree up to a point</strong>" if you agree with part of the person's opinion, but disagree with another part. Another expression is "<strong>I agree in principle</strong>" if you agree with the idea, but not with the practical application.

Avoid the common error of saying "I'm agree." The correct phrase is "<strong>I agree</strong>" (more informally) or "<strong>I'm in agreement</strong>" (more formally).

We can say that two people <strong>share an opinion</strong> or <strong>share a view</strong> if they have the same perspective. But if there are differences, then we call this a <strong>difference of opinion</strong>. If the difference of opinion is very big, then the two sides are said to be "<strong>worlds apart</strong>" on the issue. Hopefully, two people who think differently will be able to <strong>reach a compromise</strong> (agree on a fair solution) without having a <strong>heated argument</strong> (angry/intense argument) about the issue.

One polite way to express your disagreement is: "<strong>I'm afraid I disagree</strong>." You can also say "<strong>I see your point</strong>" to communicate the fact that you understand the other person's idea or opinion. We call a small disagreement a <strong>minor/slight disagreement</strong>, and a big disagreement is a <strong>major/serious disagreement</strong>. It's easy to reason with a person who disagrees respectfully, but it's more difficult to <strong>settle a disagreement</strong> (resolve a disagreement) if the other person <strong>disagrees vehemently</strong> (with intense emotion and expression).

If a conflict continues for a long time, it can be called a <strong>long-running dispute</strong> – and if it involves lots of angry and negative emotions, then it's a <strong>bitter dispute</strong>. If the two sides cannot <strong>agree to disagree</strong> (tolerate the other person's opinion, despite disagreeing), then they may need the help of a third party to resolve the conflict.
