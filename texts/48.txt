Lesson 48 – Metaphor

A number of words in English are used metaphorically – with a meaning that is not exactly the same as their literal meaning. Usually they apply a quality of one word to another word. For example, when we say that something is <strong>lightning fast</strong> (extremely fast), we are using the quality of lightning (which is almost instantaneous) to describe something that is extremely fast.

Another example is the <strong>root of the problem</strong> (the deepest, most basic or fundamental reason for the problem) – the literal meaning of the word "root" is the part of a plant or tree that is under the soil. The roots are what support and sustain the entire tree. Therefore, "the root of the problem" is the deepest, most basic or fundamental reason for the problem.

Many metaphors are related to elements in the natural world, such as light and dark, water, fire, and plants – in this lesson, you'll learn 5 collocations in each category.

**LIGHT AND DARK**

If someone's face or eyes <strong>light up</strong> (suddenly get very happy or excited), it means that they suddenly get very happy or excited. You can also say someone's eyes are <strong>shining with delight</strong> (showing happiness in their eyes).

The expression <strong>lighten the mood</strong> (make a depressing atmosphere more relaxed and happy) means to make a depressing atmosphere/situation more relaxed and happy.

On the other hand, if someone's face/eyes/expression <strong>darkens</strong> (suddenly appear sad or angry), it means that they suddenly appear sad or angry.

<strong>Dark days</strong> or <strong>dark times</strong> (periods of time that are bad or unpleasant) refer to periods of time that are bad or unpleasant, full of problems.

**WATER**

You can say that ideas are <strong>flowing</strong> (proceeding easily and naturally) or conversation is <strong>flowing</strong> to describe when it is proceeding easily and naturally.

Water metaphors are often used to describe the movement of groups of people – you can say that people <strong>poured/streamed into</strong> (continuous movement of large groups) the stadium (to describe continuous movement of large groups of people) or that people <strong>trickled into</strong> (when only a few people come, occasionally) the church (when only a few people come, occasionally).

The expressions <strong>waves of disappointment</strong> or <strong>waves of sadness</strong> (strong feelings) refer to strong feelings.

**FIRE**

A <strong>heated discussion</strong> or <strong>heated debate</strong> (very intense discussion, often with negative emotions) is one that is very intense, often with negative emotions like anger.

If someone has a <strong>fiery temper</strong> (when they get angry, they get VERY angry), it means that when they get angry, they get VERY angry, and could possibly explode. There's also the expression <strong>tempers flared</strong> (people began to get angry) to describe a situation when people began to get angry.

The verbs <strong>kindle</strong> and <strong>spark</strong> describe the act of starting fires. "Kindle" is usually used for starting positive things – something can <strong>kindle interest/enthusiasm/hope</strong> (start positive feelings) – and "spark" is usually used for starting negative things – something can <strong>spark outrage/controversy</strong> (start negative situations).

**PLANTS**

It's difficult for a plant to grow in soil that has a lot of rocks. So if something gets off to a <strong>rocky start</strong> (encountered many problems in the beginning), it means that it encountered many problems in the beginning.

Thorns are the small, sharp growths that appear on some plants (such as roses). The expression a <strong>thorny issue</strong> (a matter that is difficult, complicated, and possibly dangerous) describes a matter that is difficult, complicated, and possibly dangerous.

The word "blossom" is used to describe when a flower appears and opens, revealing all its beauty. So if a romance/friendship is <strong>blossoming</strong> (developing in a beautiful way), then it is developing in a beautiful way.

If your efforts begin to produce the desired results, we can say your efforts are <strong>bearing fruit</strong> (producing the desired results).

A seed represents something very small that can grow into something big. So if some event <strong>plants seeds of hope/jealousy</strong> (produces a small feeling that can grow into a stronger emotion), it means it produces a small feeling that can grow into a stronger emotion over time.
