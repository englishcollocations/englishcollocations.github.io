<ol>
  <li><strong>accusations of fraud</strong> – formal charges or claims of dishonest business practices.<br><em>Example:</em> The company's executives faced serious accusations of fraud from whistleblowers.</li>
  <li><strong>openly accused</strong> – publicly and directly blamed someone for wrongdoing.<br><em>Example:</em> The senator openly accused his opponent of accepting illegal campaign contributions.</li>
  <li><strong>denies the allegations</strong> – refuses to accept or states that accusations are false.<br><em>Example:</em> The athlete denies the allegations of using performance-enhancing drugs.</li>
  <li><strong>substantiate his claims</strong> – provide evidence or proof to support assertions.<br><em>Example:</em> The journalist was unable to substantiate his claims about government corruption.</li>
  <li><strong>leaked document</strong> – confidential information that was accidentally or deliberately made public.<br><em>Example:</em> The leaked document revealed the company's secret merger plans.</li>
  <li><strong>heavily implies</strong> – strongly suggests something without stating it directly.<br><em>Example:</em> His speech heavily implies that major changes are coming to the organization.</li>
  <li><strong>spreading the rumor</strong> – passing along unconfirmed information or gossip.<br><em>Example:</em> Social media users began spreading the rumor about the celebrity's secret marriage.</li>
  <li><strong>widely acknowledged</strong> – generally accepted or recognized by many people.<br><em>Example:</em> It is widely acknowledged that education is key to economic development.</li>
  <li><strong>fueled speculation</strong> – increased or encouraged theories and guesses.<br><em>Example:</em> The CEO's sudden resignation fueled speculation about the company's financial health.</li>
  <li><strong>ulterior motive</strong> – a hidden or secret reason for doing something.<br><em>Example:</em> She suspected her colleague had an ulterior motive for volunteering to help.</li>
  <li><strong>issued a statement</strong> – officially released a formal declaration or announcement.<br><em>Example:</em> The government issued a statement condemning the terrorist attack.</li>
  <li><strong>unfounded</strong> – without basis in fact or evidence; not supported by proof.<br><em>Example:</em> The police determined that the theft accusations were completely unfounded.</li>
  <li><strong>damage to his reputation</strong> – harm to someone's good name or standing in the community.<br><em>Example:</em> The scandal caused irreparable damage to the politician's reputation.</li>
</ol>
